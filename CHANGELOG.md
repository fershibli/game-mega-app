# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/) and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [2.0.0] - Unreleased

### Added
- **Nonogram** (paint by numbers) on 5×5, 10×10 and 15×15 grids: more than 20 hand-drawn pictures for each size (animals, flowers, a house, a teapot...), revealed in color with their name, every one solvable line by line. Tap to paint, hold or **Mark** for ✕, drag to paint a line; finished lines dim, Undo and Hint.
- **Kakuro**, the crossword of numbers, on three grid sizes: every puzzle has one solution found by reasoning alone. Notes, Undo, and a Hint that first shows the combinations that make the sums.
- **Killer sudoku** (sudoku with sums): dashed cages with their totals, unique solutions checked by a solver; Easy has single-square cages and needs simple reasoning, Hard has bigger cages and may need the 45 rule. Notes, Undo, and a Hint that shows the cage combinations.
- **Word search**: find 6, 8 or 10 words in an 8×8 to 12×12 grid by sliding a finger (or tapping the first and last letter); Easy reads only across and down, Medium adds diagonals, Hard adds backwards. Found words get colored bands with their own patterns (clear in color-blind mode too), clues on tap, and a Hint that circles where a word starts and ends.
- **Anagrams**: rounds of five scrambled words to rebuild with big tiles, with Undo, Shuffle and a Hint that places the next right letter; Easy shows the clue and the first letter, Medium the clue, Hard nothing until asked.
- **Word of the day**: guess a hidden word on notebook lines, each letter marked ● right place, ○ elsewhere or — not in the word (symbols first, so it never depends on colors), one word per day, level and language, then practice words on demand. Japanese and Chinese pick kana or characters from a small bank.
- The three word games work in all ten languages with the crossword dictionaries, which gained some 60 Chinese words of three and four characters (including well-known four-character sayings).
- **Checkers** with five sets of rules (Brazilian, International 10×10, English, Italian, Russian): mandatory and majority captures, flying kings where they apply, multi-captures by tapping the final square, a draw after long king-only endgames, Undo and Hint, and a computer opponent on three levels.
- **Chess** against the computer with every rule (castling, en passant, promotion, all the draws), Undo and Hint, plus **Famous games**: seven historic games, from the Immortal Game (1851) to Kasparov–Topalov (1999), replayed move by move with their story.
- **Cacheta** (cards): the Brazilian rummy against 1 to 3 computer players, with Classic, With wild card (vira and curinga) and Pife (sequences turn the corner, grab a discard to go out) rules; a fanned hand with groups underlined, sort by suit or number, a Hint and a Bater! button that lights up when every card fits.
- **Truco** (cards): Paulista (vira and manilhas) and Mineiro (fixed manilhas) rules, 1 vs 1 or 2 vs 2 with a computer partner; Truco!/Seis!/Nove!/Doze! in big speech balloons with big answer buttons, hand of eleven, and a computer that weighs its chances without seeing your cards and bluffs now and then.
- **Take turns on this phone** ("Revezando celular"): games that can be played by several people offer it at the end of the difficulty wheel, now titled "How do you want to play?", with a picture of two hands holding the phone and a line that explains it. Between turns a cover hides the player's private things and asks to pass the phone to the next player; a tap dims the scene, switches to the next player's view and, after a short pause, shows their side. Reversi is the first game with it. On tablets it says "tablet".
- **How many at the table?**: games with several table sizes ask before starting, with the last choice preselected.
- Settings → **Pace and movement**: waiting time (quick, normal, calm) between moves and before messages go away, and animations (off, gentle, full). Every game follows them.
- **Magic cube** (puzzles): the color cube puzzle in 2×2, 3×3, 4×4 and 5×5, drawn in 3D in code. Swipe a sticker or use big face buttons with ↺/↻ to turn, arrows to see every side, unlimited Undo, a step-by-step Hint that teaches the beginner's layer-by-layer method on the 2×2 and 3×3 (and walks back to the start on the bigger cubes), and a symbol on every color in the color-blind palette.
- **Block puzzle** (puzzles): place three offered pieces on a 9 × 9 or 10 × 10 board and clear full rows and columns; no clock and nothing falls, with a drag preview of where the piece lands and which lines clear, a hint, undo and, on Easy, turning pieces. The best score of each level is kept.
- **Color drops** (puzzles): a slow pair of round drops in a narrow well; three or more of one color pop and chains score more. Every color has its own symbol, Easy shows the next pair and waits while a finger rests on the buttons, and Pause freezes everything.
- **Snake** (calm action): a slow snake steered with four big arrows or swipes; on Easy it passes through the edges and touching itself only shortens the tail, Hard has walls. It always waits for Start or Continue and pauses by itself when the settings open or the app goes away.
- Three one-player card games: **Spider** (one, two or four suits; complete runs leave the table on their own, and a clear message when a new row cannot be dealt), **Free Cells** (every deal can be won; Easy deals need only two of the four free cells, Hard ones need all four; the hint comes from a solver that also tells when a game can no longer be won) and **Pyramid** (pairs that add up to 13, one, three or unlimited turns of the deck by level, every deal can be cleared). All with tap to move (tap again to try another place), Undo, Hint and a "no more moves" way out.
- **Go** on 9×9, 13×13 or 19×19 boards (pinch to zoom): captures, no suicide, simple ko, a Pass button, Chinese area scoring with 6.5 komi, and after two passes the computer proposes which groups are dead and shows the territory (tap a group to change it, or keep playing). Easy plays almost at random but takes stones in atari; Medium and Hard use a Monte Carlo tree search that never fills its own eyes. Undo, Hint, last move marked, captured stones counted.
- **Five in a row** (Gomoku, free style on a 15×15 board, five or more wins), with a second tap to confirm each stone, and a computer that searches threats (fours and open threes) with alpha-beta; Undo and Hint.
- **Four in a row** on a wooden stand with cream and dark discs that drop into place: tap anywhere in a column. The computer searches with alpha-beta and is very strong on Hard; Undo and Hint.
- **Ludo** for 2 to 4 players against one, two or three computer players: tap the big die, then a highlighted piece (single moves play themselves), a symbol on every color, classic rules (safe star squares, a 6 rolls again, three 6s lose the turn) or simple rules, Undo and Hint, three levels.
- **Nine Men’s Morris** with six and twelve men’s variants (the twelve with diagonals): placing, moving and jumping with three pieces, mills that remove a piece, Undo and Hint, and a computer opponent on three levels.
- **Mancala** with Kalah and Oware (abapa) rules: seeds drawn as pebbles with a big count on every pit, a short sowing animation, extra turns and captures, the grand slam and feeding rules, Undo and Hint, three levels.
- **Guess the word** (puzzles): find a hidden word letter by letter while a drawn flower keeps its petals; every letter that is not in the word lets one petal drift gently to the grass, a found word makes the flower open wide under the sun, and a missed word is simply shown before a new flower is planted. Rounds of five words from the crossword dictionaries with their clues, 8, 6 or 5 petals by level (Easy also gives one letter, Hard hides the clue until Hint), a keyboard per language and a kana or character bank in Japanese and Chinese.
- **Cryptogram** (puzzles): some 60 traditional proverbs per language written in code, every letter swapped for another (never itself), or for a number on Easy; tap a square and a letter and it fills every square with the same symbol. Three, one or no letters revealed by level, a Hint that fixes a letter, and the crossword spelling rules (Ñ, AE/OE/UE, Ё as Е, Arabic without harakat and read from the right). Played in the eight alphabetic languages; Japanese and Chinese players are asked which of them to use.
- **Maze** (puzzles): newspaper-style perfect mazes of 8×8, 14×14 and 20×20 squares on paper with thick walls, drawn by sliding a finger along the corridors (slide back or tap the line to go back, tap further along a corridor to walk to it), a round friend at the start and a little house at the finish, pinch to zoom, no clock, and a Hint that rings the next squares or where to go back to; the whole way lights up at the end.
- Home screen grouped by category (cards, board games, puzzles, calm action, pastimes), ready for many more games. Settings → Game list: **Group by category** (or one single list) and **Recently played first** (the game you open moves to the top of its group and its group to the top; both layouts keep their own order).
- A rules screen for games with regional variants, shown before the difficulty; the rules picked last time come preselected.
- Three calm puzzles: **Peg solitaire** (English 33-hole and French 37-hole boards on a wooden board with glass marbles; Easy starts partly solved, unlimited Undo and Restart), **Find the pairs** (memory with playing cards, fruits, symbols or animals, 6 to 15 pairs, no timer, best result kept) and **Follow the colors** (repeat a growing sequence of four squares, each with its own symbol; goals of 5, 8 and 12 colors, best length kept).
- Lasting storage for pastime games (a pet, a farm), kept apart from the game in progress and included in Android's own backup.
- **Pet friend** (pastimes): adopt one of ten pixel-art friends, name it, then feed, cuddle (stroke it with a finger), kiss, wave, bathe, put it to bed, walk it in the garden, dress it up and play rock-paper-scissors, tic-tac-toe or ball with it. Four gentle bars computed from time, never below 20 %; the pet never gets sick or leaves, and is kept on the phone across restarts and other games.
- Settings → **Why Mili Games exists** (at the very end): why the app is and always will be free and ad-free, how it was made (with the help of Claude, with no AI inside the app) and a button to the source code and documentation. The same text is in the store descriptions.
- **Arabic** (Modern Standard Arabic): screens read right to left, while game boards keep their layout and left-handed mode still means the physical left; the crossword reads right to left with a letter bank. Western digits are kept, as on the boards.
- Three Asian chess games against the computer (Easy / Medium / Hard): **Chinese chess** (xiangqi), **Korean chess** (janggi, with Pass) and **Shogi** (standard 9 × 9 or 5 × 5 mini shogi, with drops and promotion); every piece shows its traditional character plus marks showing how it moves, and a Pieces guide explains each one.
- Games that cannot be played in every language: they stay on the home screen and, when the app language is not one of theirs, ask first which of their languages to play in (one big button each, the last choice first). The game then plays in that language, and a saved game always continues in the language it started in.
- CI reports the release APK size, warns past 10 MB and fails past 20 MB.
- Four new languages: **Italian, German, Russian and Japanese** — the interface, crossword dictionaries (with QWERTZ and ЙЦУКЕН keyboards; Japanese in katakana with a kana bank), store listings, README, user guide, trust page and privacy policy.

### Changed
- Pastimes (the pet, the farm) no longer clear the game in progress, and their settings have no "New game".
- The app is now called **Mili Games** and its package is `io.github.fershibli.miligames`. Android sees it as a different app: uninstall the previous version (the saved game is lost once) and install this one; later versions update normally. The release key is the same.

## [1.0.0] - 2026-10-19

### Added
- Ten games with Easy / Medium / Hard: Sudoku, Solitaire (Klondike), Buraco, Backgammon, Mahjong solitaire, Reversi, Crossword, Pipes, Connect and Dots.
- Procedural, always-solvable puzzles (Sudoku with unique solution, Mahjong, Pipes, Connect, Crossword).
- Computer opponents for Reversi (alpha-beta), Backgammon (heuristic + one-roll expectimax) and Buraco (heuristic).
- Home screen with endless two-column picture grid; difficulty scroll wheel with the default preselected.
- Settings: language (EN, PT-BR, ES, FR, ZH), text size, piece/button size, Normal / High-contrast / Color-blind palettes each with a dark mode, pinch zoom, left-handed mode, one-hand mode (floating gear, double-tap or press-and-rotate zoom), default difficulty.
- Automatic save with "continue where you left off?" on the next start.
- Backgammon on the traditional horizontal board, with pinch zoom to get closer; a Rotate button stands it up (bigger checkers) and the choice is remembered.
- Documentation in five languages, CI, Dependabot.
- Automatic semantic versioning from Conventional Commits (`versioning.yml`) and an APK deploy pipeline (`deploy.yml`) that tests, signs, verifies and publishes each release.
- Google Play: AAB in every release, optional upload to Play (`scripts/play-upload.sh`), store listing texts and graphics in five languages (`fastlane/metadata/android`), privacy policy (`PRIVACY.md`) and a step-by-step guide (`docs/en/google-play.md`).
- `signing-key.yml`: creates the release key, keeps it in the repository secrets, re-encrypts it when `MEGAGAMES_SIGNING_PASSWORD` changes, makes encrypted backups and exports it for Google Play.

### Fixed
- Sudoku: after the first tap on the board, selecting another cell could erase the numbers placed since then.

## Installing

Mili Games is not in an app store; it is installed from its GitHub page. This is safe as long as you download it from the official page below.

1. On the Android phone, open the browser and go to **https://github.com/fershibli/game-mega-app/releases**.
2. Under the newest release, tap **`MiliGames-vX.Y.Z.apk`** to download it.
3. Open the downloaded file (from the notification or the *Files / Downloads* app).
4. Android may say the browser is "not allowed to install unknown apps". Tap **Settings**, turn on **Allow from this source**, go back and tap **Install**.
5. Done: the **Mili Games** icon (a card, a die, a sudoku grid and two discs) appears with your other apps.

**Updating:** download the newer APK and install it over the old one. Settings are kept.

**Optional check:** each release has a `SHA256SUMS.txt`. A computer-savvy relative can compare it with the file (`sha256sum MiliGames-*.apk`) to be sure the file was not altered.

Requirements: Android 6.0 or newer, about 5 MB of free space. The app never needs internet.

## Getting around

The app has only three kinds of screens.

1. **Choose your game.** A grid of pictures, two per row. There is no text on purpose: each picture *is* the game (a sudoku grid, cards, a backgammon board…). The list scrolls endlessly in both directions — keep scrolling and the games come round again. Tap a picture to choose it.
2. **Choose the difficulty.** A short explanation of the game, and below it a scroll wheel with **Easy / Medium / Hard**. Your default difficulty is already selected. Turn the wheel (or tap a level) and press **Confirm**. **Back** returns to the games.
3. **The game.** The game name is at the top and the **gear ⚙** is in the top-right corner, exactly where it was on the first screen. Game buttons (undo, hint…) are at the bottom, near your thumb.

The **gear** opens the settings. Inside a game, settings also show three big buttons at the bottom:

- **Back to game** — closes the settings.
- **New game** — starts a new game of the same kind (asks for confirmation).
- **Main menu** — leaves the game and returns to the game list. *This abandons the game*: it asks "Leave this game?" first, so it cannot happen by accident.

The phone's **Back** button/gesture inside a game opens (or closes) the settings, so nobody leaves a game by mistake.

## Playing together on one phone

Some games can also be played by people sitting together, taking turns on the same phone. On the difficulty wheel, now titled **How do you want to play?**, choose **Take turns on this phone** (on a tablet: *…on this tablet*). Nobody plays against the computer.

Games you can play this way: Reversi, Checkers, Chess, Backgammon, Chinese chess, Korean chess, Shogi, Go, Gomoku, Four in a row, Nine Men’s Morris, Mancala, Ludo. In Backgammon each player rolls their own dice; Ludo is for 2 to 4 people; Chess takes turns in a normal game, not with the famous games. In these board games everything is in view of both players, so the cover between turns only says who plays next. **Undo** takes back one move, and at the end of Go both players agree on the same screen which stones are dead.

When a turn ends, the screen covers what belongs to that player (for example the cards in hand) and shows a big button with two hands holding a phone, **Pass to the next player** and who that is (a color or a number). The next person taps the button: the scene dims, switches to their side of the table and, after a short pause (see *Waiting time* in the settings), shows their things. The board never turns around, so nobody gets dizzy.

Games that can be played with different numbers of people ask **How many at the table?** first; the last choice comes preselected.

## Automatic save and "continue?"

Every move is saved immediately on the phone. If the app is closed — by accident, by a phone call, by the battery running out — the next time it opens it goes straight to that game and asks:

> **Welcome back!** You have a game of *Sudoku* in progress. Do you want to continue where you left off or go back to the main menu?

- **Continue playing** — everything is exactly as it was, including the computer's turn if it was playing.
- **Main menu** — the saved game is discarded.

Only one game is kept at a time. A finished game (won or lost) is not kept.

## Zoom

Every game board can be enlarged.

- **Pinch to zoom** (on by default): put two fingers on the board and spread them apart to enlarge, pinch to shrink. Move the enlarged board with two fingers. One finger always plays normally.
- A **reset** button (four corners) appears in the lower corner while zoomed; tap it to go back to the normal size.
- In **one-hand mode** two fingers are not needed, see below.

The game (its rules and its texts) is then played in the language you choose, while the rest of the app stays in yours. The language you picked comes first next time, and a saved game always continues in the language it was started in.

## Accessibility

Designed with older adults in mind:

- **Big targets** — buttons are at least 56 dp (about 9 mm) high, larger than Android's guideline.
- **Large type by default**, adjustable up to 160 %, on top of the phone's own font size.
- **No reading required to choose a game**, but every picture has a spoken name for **TalkBack**.
- **Thumb-friendly** — actions at the bottom, mirrored in left-handed mode, floating gear in one-hand mode.
- **Never color alone** — in High contrast and Color blind modes, pieces carry shapes (circle, square, triangle…), cards use four suit colors, and outlines get thicker.
- **Forgiving** — unlimited undo in most games, hints everywhere, no timers, blocked Mahjong tiles are dimmed, legal moves are highlighted in Reversi and Backgammon.
- **Calm** — no sounds, no flashing, no ads, no pop-ups.

## The games

### Sudoku

**Goal:** fill the 9×9 grid so every row, column and 3×3 box contains 1 to 9 once.

**Playing:** tap a square, then a number at the bottom. Tap the eraser to clear. **Notes** switches the number pad to pencil marks (small reminders); placing a number removes that note from the same row, column and box automatically. **Hint** fills the selected square (or a random one) with the right number. **Undo** steps back.

Helpers: the row, column and box of the selected square are shaded, equal numbers are highlighted, and a number button fades when all nine are placed.

| Level | Starting numbers | Mistakes shown in red |
|---|---|---|
| Easy | 40 | yes |
| Medium | 32 | yes |
| Hard | 26 | only rule conflicts |

Every puzzle is generated new and has exactly **one** solution.

### Solitaire

**Goal:** Klondike — move all cards to the four foundation piles (top), from Ace to King, one suit per pile.

**Playing:** just **tap a card**. It moves to the best place: a foundation if possible, otherwise onto a column (alternating colors, one rank lower), otherwise to an empty column (kings only). If it cannot move, it flashes red. Tapping a face-up card in a column moves it together with the cards on top of it. Tap the **deck** (top right, or top left in left-handed mode) to draw; when it is empty, tap it to turn the waste over again. **Hint** outlines a useful move; **Finish** appears when every card is face up and plays the rest for you.

| Level | Draw | Passes through the deck |
|---|---|---|
| Easy | 1 card | unlimited |
| Medium | 3 cards | unlimited |
| Hard | 3 cards | 3 |

### Buraco

Brazilian Buraco for two (you against the computer) or for four in pairs (you and a computer partner against two computers), or taken in turns on one phone. Full rules, the four-player game and tips: [Buraco](games/buraco.md).

**Cards:** two 52-card decks (104 cards). **Every 2 is wild** ("curinga").

**Deal:** 11 cards each; two extra piles of 11 cards, the **mortos**, wait on the side; one card starts the discard pile ("lixo").

**Your turn:**

1. **Draw** one card from the deck **or take the whole discard pile** (buttons at the bottom, or tap the deck/pile).
2. Optionally **lay down sequences**: select 3 or more cards of the **same suit in a row** (e.g. 5♥ 6♥ 7♥) and press **New sequence**. The ace can be low (A-2-3) or high (Q-K-A). A sequence may contain **one** wild 2; a 2 of the same suit in its own place (A-2-3♥) counts as a normal card.
3. Optionally **add cards** to your sequences: select them and **tap the sequence**.
4. **Discard** one card (select it, press **Discard**). The turn passes to the computer.

**Canasta:** a sequence of 7 or more cards. **Clean** (no wild) has a gold border and is worth 200; **dirty** (with a wild) has a silver border and is worth 100.

**Morto:** the first time your hand runs out, you pick up a morto and keep playing.

**Going out ("bater"):** you can empty your hand only after you have your morto (or none is left) **and** have at least one **clean canasta**. The app tells you if a move is not allowed and why ("keep a card to discard", "you need a clean canasta").

**End and scoring:** the game ends when someone goes out or the deck runs out.

| Item | Points |
|---|---|
| A in a sequence | 15 |
| 2 | 10 |
| 8, 9, 10, J, Q, K | 10 |
| 3 to 7 | 5 |
| Clean canasta | +200 |
| Dirty canasta | +100 |
| Going out | +100 |
| Cards left in hand | minus their value |
| Never picked up a morto | −100 |

**Undo** takes back everything you laid down since your draw. The computer's levels: *Easy* plays casually; *Medium* takes the pile when useful, protects its wild cards and avoids discarding cards that fit your sequences; *Hard* also weighs the whole pile, keeps clean canastas clean, and remembers what you picked up.

### Backgammon

**Goal:** move your 15 light checkers around the board into your home (points 1–6, bottom right) and bear them all off before the computer (dark checkers).

**Take turns on this phone:** two people can also play against each other, without the computer. The board never turns around and the screen says whose turn it is. Each player rolls their own dice.

The board is the traditional **horizontal** one: points 13→24 along the top, 12→1 along the bottom (your home at the bottom right), the **bar** down the middle and the **tray** for borne-off checkers on the right (yours at the bottom). Pinch to zoom in and move around it. **Rotate** stands the board up instead, filling the screen with bigger checkers: points 12→1 then run down the left column (your home at the bottom left), 13→24 down the right column, the bar crosses the middle and the tray is at the bottom. The app remembers your choice.

**Playing:** tap **Roll dice**. Checkers that can move glow **yellow**; tap one and its possible destinations glow **green**; tap a destination. If a checker has only one possible destination it moves straight away. When all dice are used, tap **Done**. **Undo** resets your turn. To bear off, tap the tray.

All standard rules apply: you must use both dice if possible (the larger one if only one can be played), doubles are played four times, a single opposing checker ("blot") can be hit and sent to the bar, and checkers on the bar must enter first. A win is a *gammon* (double) if the loser has borne off nothing, and a *backgammon* (triple) if the loser still has a checker on the bar or in the winner's home. There is no doubling cube.

Computer levels: *Easy* often plays a random legal move; *Medium* judges every possible play (race, safe points, blots, primes, anchors); *Hard* also looks one roll ahead, averaging over all 21 possible replies.

### Mahjong

**Goal:** remove all tiles in matching pairs.

A tile is **free** when no tile lies on top of it and its left **or** right side is open. Tap two free, identical tiles to remove them. Any two **flowers** match each other, and any two **seasons** match each other. Each suit tile shows a small Western number (or E/S/W/N for winds) in its corner, so you do not need to read Chinese.

On Easy and Medium, tiles that are **not free are dimmed**. **Hint** outlines a pair, **Undo** puts the last pair back, **Shuffle** re-deals the remaining tiles when you are stuck.

| Level | Tiles | Shuffles |
|---|---|---|
| Easy | 48 (tower) | unlimited |
| Medium | 96 (fortress) | 3 |
| Hard | 144 (classic turtle) | 1 |

Every deal is built so that it **can always be solved**.

### Reversi

**Goal:** have more discs than the computer when the board is full. You play the dark discs and start.

Place a disc so that one or more of the computer's discs are trapped in a straight line between your new disc and another of yours: they flip to your color. Possible moves are shown as small dots; the computer's last move has a red dot. If someone cannot move, they pass (the app tells you).

**Hint** marks a good move. **Undo** takes back your last move. Computer levels: depth-1 with random mistakes / depth-3 search / depth-6 search that plays the last 12 moves perfectly.

### Crossword

A **new crossword is generated** for every game from a dictionary of everyday words with gentle clues, in the app's language.

**Playing:** tap a square to choose its word; tap the same square again to switch between across and down. The clue is shown at the bottom between ◀ and ▶ (previous / next word). Type with the letters below: after each letter the cursor moves on. The eraser clears a letter. **Hint** fills in the selected square.

Accents are ignored as in printed crosswords (Ç = C, É = E…); Spanish keeps Ñ, German writes Ä Ö Ü ß as AE OE UE SS and Russian writes Ё as Е. In Chinese each square holds one character; in Japanese one katakana, with small kana written full size (ッ → ツ); in Arabic one letter without harakat (أ إ آ written as ا), and across words read right to left.

| Level | Grid | Input | Errors in red |
|---|---|---|---|
| Easy | 9×9 (8×8 in Chinese and Japanese), ~7 words (~6) | letter bank (word letters + 2 extra) | yes |
| Medium | 11×11 (10×10), ~11 words (~9) | full keyboard in the language's layout (Chinese, Japanese and Arabic: bank with 5 extra) | yes |
| Hard | 13×13 (12×12), ~16 words (~12) | full keyboard (Chinese, Japanese and Arabic: bank with 8 extra) | no |

French uses an AZERTY keyboard, German QWERTZ, Russian ЙЦУКЕН and the others QWERTY.

### Pipes

**Goal:** turn the pieces so that water from the round source in the middle reaches **every** piece, with no pipe pointing at a wall or a closed side.

Tap a piece to turn it a quarter turn clockwise. Pieces with water turn blue; end pieces are little tanks that fill up. **Hint** turns one wrong piece into place. **Undo** reverses turns. Grid: 5×5 / 7×7 / 9×9. The puzzle is a random tree of pipes, so there are no loops and a solution always exists.

### Connect

**Goal:** draw lines that join each pair of dots of the same color. Lines cannot cross.

Put your finger on a dot and draw through the squares (up, down, left, right). Drawing over another line cuts it. Start on a line to shorten it. **Hint** draws one correct line; **Clear** erases all lines.

| Level | Board | Colors | To win |
|---|---|---|---|
| Easy | 5×5 | 4 | connect all colors |
| Medium | 7×7 | 6 | connect all colors and fill every square |
| Hard | 9×9 | 8 | connect all colors and fill every square |

Puzzles are generated by cutting a random path that covers the whole board, so each one can be completely filled.

### Dots

**Goal:** reach the targets shown at the top (e.g. collect 15 red, 15 blue and 15 green dots) before you run out of moves.

Drag across **neighboring dots of the same color** (up, down, left, right; at least two). When you lift your finger they disappear and new dots fall from the top. **Close a square** (come back to a dot already in your line) to clear **every dot of that color** — the board glows when you have a square. Moves turn red when 3 or fewer remain. Easy: 6×6, 4 colors, 22 moves. Medium: 7×7, 5 colors, 20 moves. Hard: 8×8, 5 colors, 18 moves, four targets.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does it use mobile data or Wi-Fi?** No. The app has no internet permission at all; Android blocks every connection.

**Does it collect any data?** No. Settings and the current game are stored only on the phone, in the app's private storage. Android's own backup (if you enabled it) may copy them to *your* Google account.

**Why is it not on the Play Store?** It may be in the future. For now, releases are published on GitHub with checksums.

**The text is too small / too big.** Settings → Size → Text size. The phone's own font size setting is also respected.

**Can I play two games at the same time?** Only one game is saved. Starting a new game replaces the saved one.

**Is the computer cheating?** No. The computer sees only what a human would (its own hand, the table, the discard pile) and uses the same dice/shuffles. Its code is open: see [technical.md](technical.md#computer-opponents).

**I found a problem or I have an idea.** Please open an issue: https://github.com/fershibli/game-mega-app/issues

# Checkers

🌐 **English** · [Português](../../pt-BR/games/checkers.md) · [Español](../../es/games/checkers.md) · [Français](../../fr/games/checkers.md) · [Italiano](../../it/games/checkers.md) · [Deutsch](../../de/games/checkers.md) · [Русский](../../ru/games/checkers.md) · [日本語](../../ja/games/checkers.md) · [中文](../../zh-CN/games/checkers.md) · [العربية](../../ar/games/checkers.md)

A board game for two: you against the computer. Pieces move diagonally on the dark squares; you capture by jumping over an enemy piece onto the empty square just behind it. A piece that reaches the far side becomes a **king** (it gets a gold crown). You win when the computer has no pieces left or cannot move.

**Take turns on this phone:** two people can also play against each other, without the computer. The board never turns around and the screen says whose turn it is.

## How to play

- Tap one of your pieces: the squares it can reach light up. Tap one of them.
- **Capturing is mandatory.** When you can capture, the pieces that can do it are circled.
- To take several pieces in one move, tap the final square. If there is more than one way to get there, tap each landing square in turn.
- **Undo** takes back your last move (and the computer's answer). **Hint** shows a good move.
- If for 25 moves each only kings move and nothing is captured, the game is a draw, so it never gets stuck.

## The rules you can choose

- **Brazilian checkers** (8×8, 12 pieces each): men move forwards one square and capture forwards and backwards. Kings "fly": they move and capture any distance along a diagonal. You must take the largest number of pieces possible. A man that reaches the last row in the middle of a capture is only crowned if it ends its move there.
- **International draughts** (10×10, 20 pieces each): the same rules as Brazilian checkers on a bigger board.
- **English checkers** (8×8, also called American checkers): men move and capture only forwards; kings move one square in any diagonal direction. Capturing is mandatory, but you may choose which capture to make. A man that is crowned ends its move. Dark moves first, so here you play the dark pieces.
- **Italian checkers** (8×8, board turned with a playing square in your right-hand corner): men capture only forwards and can never capture a king; kings move one square. You must take the most pieces; if there is a tie, capture with a king, then take the most kings.
- **Russian checkers** (8×8): men capture backwards too, kings fly, and you may choose any capture. A man crowned in the middle of a capture goes on capturing as a king.

## Levels

- **Easy**: looks two moves ahead and now and then makes a careless move. Good for learning.
- **Medium**: looks about four moves ahead.
- **Hard**: thinks as far as it can in under a second.

## Tips

- Keep your back row in place as long as you can: it stops the computer from getting kings.
- Pieces in the middle of the board are stronger than pieces on the edges.
- Before moving, check whether your piece can be jumped on the next move.

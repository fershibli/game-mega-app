# Cacheta

Cacheta is the Brazilian rummy game played at so many kitchen tables: draw, throw away, and be the first to have every card in a group. In Mili Games you play against **1, 2 or 3 computer players** (you choose at the start of each game).

## The cards
- Two decks of 52 cards (104 cards), no jokers.
- Everyone gets **9 cards**. The rest is the **deck**; the **discard pile** ("lixo") starts empty. You play first.

## Your turn
1. **Draw** the top card of the deck, **or take** the top card of the discard pile (buttons at the bottom, or tap the deck or the pile).
2. **Discard** one card: tap it in your hand (it rises) and press **Discard**. A card you just took from the pile cannot be thrown back at once.

## Groups
- **Sequence** ("sequência"): 3 or more cards of the **same suit in a row**, such as 5♥ 6♥ 7♥.
- **Trio** ("trinca"): 3 or 4 cards of the **same number, all of different suits**, such as 9♣ 9♦ 9♥.
- A **green bar** under your cards shows the ones that already form groups.

## Going out ("bater")
After drawing you hold 10 cards. When 9 of them form groups (the 10th is your last discard), or all 10 do, the **Bater!** button lights up: press it and you win the hand. Going out with all 10 cards is the famous "batida de dez". If somebody else goes out first, you lose that hand. If the deck runs out, the discard pile is shuffled into a new deck.

**Scoring:** at the table people often start with 7 or 10 "lives" and lose one each time someone else goes out. In Mili Games every hand is one game — whoever goes out wins — so you can stop whenever you like.

## Rules (variants)
- **Classic** — two decks, 9 cards, no wild cards. The ace is low (A-2-3) or high (Q-K-A), but a sequence does not turn the corner (K-A-2 is not allowed).
- **With wild card** — after dealing, one card is turned face up beside the deck: the **vira**. The card of the **same suit one number above** is the **wild card** ("curinga"): with two decks there are two of them. A wild card (marked with a star) replaces any card in a sequence or trio; a group still needs one normal card. Example: vira 4♥ → the 5♥ is wild. A king turned up makes the ace wild.
- **Pife** — no wild cards; sequences may turn the corner (Q-K-A-2), and **you may grab any card thrown away to go out**, even out of turn ("furar a vez"): when a computer discards the card you need, you are asked "Take it?" and can press **Bater!**. The computers can do the same to you!

## Levels
- **Easy:** the computers keep sensible cards but throw away fairly random ones, take the pile only now and then and sometimes do not notice they could go out.
- **Medium:** they keep the cards that form or almost form groups and discard the most useless one.
- **Hard:** they also avoid throwing away cards close to the ones the next player picked up from the pile.

## Tips
- **Hint** suggests the card to discard. **By suit / By number** sorts your hand.
- Pairs (two 7s, or 7♥ 8♥) are halfway to a group: keep them and throw away lonely high cards first.
- Watch the pile: if a computer picked up a 6♠, do not feed it the 5♠ or 7♠.

# Nine Men’s Morris

🌐 **English** · [Português](../../pt-BR/games/morris.md) · [Español](../../es/games/morris.md) · [Français](../../fr/games/morris.md) · [Italiano](../../it/games/morris.md) · [Deutsch](../../de/games/morris.md) · [Русский](../../ru/games/morris.md) · [日本語](../../ja/games/morris.md) · [中文](../../zh-CN/games/morris.md) · [العربية](../../ar/games/morris.md)

One of the oldest board games in the world, for two: you (light pieces) against the computer (dark pieces). The board has points joined by lines. Line up three of your pieces along a line, a **mill**, and you remove one of the computer’s pieces.

**Take turns on this phone:** two people can also play against each other, without the computer. The board never turns around and the screen says whose turn it is.

## How to play

1. **Placing**: you take turns putting one piece on any empty point. Your pieces still to place wait below the board, the computer’s above it.
2. **Moving**: once all pieces are placed, tap one of your pieces and then a free neighboring point along a line.
3. **Mill**: whenever you make a new mill, tap one of the computer’s pieces (they are circled) to remove it. Pieces that are part of a mill cannot be removed, unless all of them are.

- **Undo** takes back your last move (and the computer’s answer). **Hint** circles a good move.
- You win when the computer has only **two pieces** left or **cannot move**.
- If 50 moves each go by without a mill, the game is a draw.

## The rules you can choose

- **Nine men’s morris** (the classic): 9 pieces each on three squares. A player reduced to **three pieces may jump** (fly) to any empty point.
- **Six men’s morris**: 6 pieces each on a smaller board with two squares. Mills only along the sides; no jumping.
- **Twelve men’s morris**: 12 pieces each, and diagonal lines join the corners; mills can also be made along them. With three pieces you may jump. If the board fills up during placing, the game is a draw.

## Levels

- **Easy**: looks one move ahead and now and then plays at random.
- **Medium**: looks about three moves ahead.
- **Hard**: thinks as far as it can in under a second.

## Tips

- During placing, keep your pieces free to move: crowded corners get blocked later.
- Two pieces on a line with the third point empty threaten a mill; block the computer’s.
- A “double mill” (a piece that opens one mill and closes another by moving back and forth) wins pieces every turn.

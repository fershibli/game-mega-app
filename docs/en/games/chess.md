# Chess

🌐 **English** · [Português](../../pt-BR/games/chess.md) · [Español](../../es/games/chess.md) · [Français](../../fr/games/chess.md) · [Italiano](../../it/games/chess.md) · [Deutsch](../../de/games/chess.md) · [Русский](../../ru/games/chess.md) · [日本語](../../ja/games/chess.md) · [中文](../../zh-CN/games/chess.md) · [العربية](../../ar/games/chess.md)

The classic game of strategy. You play White, at the bottom of the screen; the computer plays Black. Win by **checkmating** the computer's king: attacking it so that it has no escape.

## Play against the computer

- Tap one of your pieces: the squares it can go to are marked with dots (rings when it would capture). Tap one of them.
- The last move is tinted, and a king in check is circled in red.
- All the rules are there: castling (move the king two squares), en passant, promotion (a pawn reaching the far side becomes a queen, rook, bishop or knight: big buttons, queen first), check, checkmate and stalemate.
- The game is drawn by stalemate, when neither side has enough pieces to checkmate, after 50 moves each without a capture or a pawn move, or when the same position comes up three times.
- **Undo** takes back your move and the computer's answer. **Hint** shows a good move.

## Famous games

Replay seven great games from history, move by move, with big **Start**, **Back**, **Next** and **End** buttons and the list of moves (tap any move to jump there). Each game shows the players, the place and the year, and why it is famous:

- The Immortal Game: Anderssen–Kieseritzky, London 1851
- The Evergreen Game: Anderssen–Dufresne, Berlin 1852
- The Opera Game: Morphy–Duke Karl of Brunswick and Count Isouard, Paris 1858
- Steinitz–von Bardeleben, Hastings 1895
- Réti–Tartakower, Vienna 1910
- The Game of the Century: D. Byrne–Fischer, New York 1956
- Kasparov–Topalov, Wijk aan Zee 1999

The level does not matter here.

## Levels

- **Easy**: looks one move ahead and sometimes picks a weaker move. Beatable by a casual player.
- **Medium**: looks about three moves ahead.
- **Hard**: looks four or five moves ahead and never leaves a piece hanging by accident.

## Tips

- Bring out your knights and bishops early, and castle to keep your king safe.
- Before each move, ask: what does the computer threaten?
- Moves are written in figurine notation: ♘f3 means "knight to f3", x means a capture, + check and # checkmate.

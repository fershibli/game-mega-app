# Shogi (Japanese chess)

🌐 **English** · [Português](../../pt-BR/games/shogi.md) · [Español](../../es/games/shogi.md) · [Français](../../fr/games/shogi.md) · [Italiano](../../it/games/shogi.md) · [Deutsch](../../de/games/shogi.md) · [Русский](../../ru/games/shogi.md) · [日本語](../../ja/games/shogi.md) · [中文](../../zh-CN/games/shogi.md) · [العربية](../../ar/games/shogi.md)

Shogi is the chess of Japan. Its big difference: **pieces you capture change sides**. You can later place them back on the board as your own.

**Take turns on this phone:** two people can also play against each other, without the computer. The board never turns around and the screen says whose turn it is. Each player’s captured pieces wait on their own side of the board.

## Rules to choose

- **Shogi (9 × 9)**: the full game, 20 pieces each.
- **Mini shogi (5 × 5)**: a small board with one piece of each kind (king, rook, bishop, gold, silver, pawn). Games are short: ideal to learn.

## The board and the pieces

- Pieces stand **inside the squares**. All pieces are the same color: yours **point up**, the computer's point down (and are a little darker).
- You move first, from the bottom.
- On every piece, a **dot** means one step that way and an **arrow** means any distance. Red characters are promoted pieces.

| Piece | How it moves | Promoted |
|---|---|---|
| King 王 / 玉 | One step in any direction. | — |
| Rook 飛 | Any distance along a line. | Dragon 龍: also one step diagonally. |
| Bishop 角 | Any distance diagonally. | Horse 馬: also one step along a line. |
| Gold 金 | One step in any direction except diagonally backward. | Does not promote. |
| Silver 銀 | One step forward or diagonally. | Moves like a gold (全). |
| Knight 桂 | Jumps two forward and one to the side (two dots at the top). | Moves like a gold (圭). |
| Lance 香 | Any distance straight forward. | Moves like a gold (杏). |
| Pawn 歩 | One step forward. | Tokin と: moves like a gold. |

Tap **Pieces** at any time to see the list with big pictures.

## Promotion

The three far rows (one row in mini shogi) are the **promotion zone**, softly shaded. When a piece moves into, inside or out of it, the app asks with two big buttons: **Promote** or **Keep it as it is**. If the piece could never move again otherwise (a pawn or lance on the last row, a knight on the last two rows), it promotes automatically.

## Drops

Captured pieces wait **in your hand**, under the board. Instead of moving, tap one of them and then a green square to place it there (a "drop"). Rules for drops:

- a dropped piece is never promoted at once;
- no two unpromoted pawns of yours on the same column;
- a pawn drop may not give **immediate checkmate** (other pieces may);
- no piece where it could never move (pawn or lance on the last row, knight on the last two rows).

## Winning

Checkmate the computer's king. A position repeated **four times** (sennichite) is a draw.

## Playing in the app

1. Tap a piece on the board or in your hand: green dots show where it may go.
2. Tap a green dot. Answer the promotion question when asked.
3. **Undo** takes back your last move and the computer's answer; **Hint** shows a good move.

## Levels

- **Easy**: looks one move ahead and makes many mistakes.
- **Medium**: looks a few moves ahead.
- **Hard**: thinks as deeply as it can in under a second.

## What is simplified

The rule that makes a player who gives perpetual check lose is not applied: every fourfold repetition is a draw. A game that reaches 400 moves is a draw. The "entering king" (jishogi) point count is not used.

## Tips

- Drops are powerful: a captured gold or silver dropped next to the enemy king often decides the game.
- Keep your king behind a wall of gold and silver generals.
- Promote whenever you can, except for the rare cases when the unpromoted silver or knight is more useful.

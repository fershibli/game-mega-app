# Buraco

🌐 **English** · [Português](../../pt-BR/games/buraco.md) · [Español](../../es/games/buraco.md) · [Français](../../fr/games/buraco.md) · [Italiano](../../it/games/buraco.md) · [Deutsch](../../de/games/buraco.md) · [Русский](../../ru/games/buraco.md) · [日本語](../../ja/games/buraco.md) · [中文](../../zh-CN/games/buraco.md) · [العربية](../../ar/games/buraco.md)

Buraco is the Brazilian rummy of sequences and canastas, played with two decks. In Mili Games it is played by **two** (you against the computer) or by **four in pairs** ("duplas"): you and a computer partner, sitting opposite each other, against two computer players. The app asks **How many at the table?** before each game and remembers your choice. You can also **take turns on one phone** with other people.

## The cards
- Two 52-card decks (104 cards), no jokers. **Every 2 is wild** ("curinga").
- Everyone gets **11 cards**. Two extra piles of 11 cards, the **mortos**, wait on the side (one for each side of the table), and one card starts the discard pile ("lixo").

## Your turn
1. **Draw** one card from the deck, **or take the whole discard pile**.
2. If you like, **lay down sequences**: 3 or more cards of the **same suit in a row** (5♥ 6♥ 7♥), with **New sequence**. The ace is low (A-2-3) or high (Q-K-A). A sequence may hold **one** wild 2; a 2 of the same suit in its own place (A-2-3♥) is a normal card.
3. If you like, **add cards** to your side's sequences: select them and tap the sequence.
4. **Discard** one card. **Undo** takes back everything you laid down since your draw.

**Canasta:** 7 or more cards. **Clean** (no wild card, gold border) is worth 200; **dirty** (with a wild card, silver border) 100.

**Morto:** the first time a hand runs out, that player picks up the side's morto and keeps playing (or, after a discard, plays it next turn).

**Going out ("bater"):** a hand may run out for good only when the side already has its morto (or none is left) **and** at least one **clean canasta**. The game also ends when the deck runs out.

## Scoring
| Item | Points |
|---|---|
| A | 15 |
| 2, and 8 to K | 10 |
| 3 to 7 | 5 |
| Clean canasta | +200 |
| Dirty canasta | +100 |
| Going out | +100 |
| Cards left in hand | minus their value |
| Never picked up the morto | −100 |

## Four players in pairs
- **Seats:** you at the bottom, your **partner** across, the opponents on your **left** and **right**. Each seat has its own color and symbol (● you, ▲ right, ■ partner, ◆ left); the player whose turn it is gets a bright frame, and every seat shows how many cards it holds.
- **Order of play:** counterclockwise, as is the custom in Brazil: after you, the player on your **right**, then your partner, then the player on your left.
- **One table per side:** you and your partner lay down and add to the **same sequences** ("Our sequences"); the opponents share theirs. Each side picks up **one morto**: whoever of the two empties a hand first takes it.
- **Scores per side:** the bar at the top shows the points each side already has on the table (cards and canastas). At the end, each side scores its sequences and canastas, +100 if one of the two went out, minus the cards left in **both** hands, and −100 if it never took its morto.
- **Your computer partner** adds to your shared sequences, keeps clean canastas clean on Hard, and avoids throwing away cards the next opponent could use.

## Taking turns on one phone
Choose **Take turns on this phone** on the level screen, then 2 or 4 at the table. Everybody is a person (2 people, or 4 people in pairs: players 1 and 3 against players 2 and 4). After each discard a cover hides the cards and asks to pass the phone to the next player, shown with their color and symbol; they tap the big button (“Ready, it is my turn”) to see their own hand.

## Levels
- **Easy:** plays casually, rarely takes the pile and discards almost at random.
- **Medium:** takes the pile when it is useful, protects its wild cards and avoids discarding cards that fit the other side's sequences.
- **Hard:** also weighs the whole pile, keeps clean canastas clean and remembers what the other side picked up.

The computer never sees your cards.

## Tips
- A clean canasta is the key to going out: do not spend your 2s too early.
- Watch the discard pile before throwing a card that extends the other side's sequences.
- With a partner, add to the shared sequences rather than starting new ones: a canasta comes sooner.

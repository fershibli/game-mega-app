# Chinese chess (Xiangqi)

🌐 **English** · [Português](../../pt-BR/games/xiangqi.md) · [Español](../../es/games/xiangqi.md) · [Français](../../fr/games/xiangqi.md) · [Italiano](../../it/games/xiangqi.md) · [Deutsch](../../de/games/xiangqi.md) · [Русский](../../ru/games/xiangqi.md) · [日本語](../../ja/games/xiangqi.md) · [中文](../../zh-CN/games/xiangqi.md) · [العربية](../../ar/games/xiangqi.md)

Chinese chess is the most played board game in China and Vietnam. Two armies face each other across a river; whoever traps the other side's **general** wins.

**Take turns on this phone:** two people can also play against each other, without the computer. The board never turns around and the screen says whose turn it is.

## The board

- 9 lines across and 10 lines down. The pieces stand **on the crossings**, not inside the squares.
- The **river** runs across the middle. Some pieces change when they cross it.
- Each side has a **palace**: the 3 × 3 crossings with an X drawn in them. The general and its two advisors never leave it.
- You play **red**, at the bottom, and move first. The computer plays black.

## The pieces and their marks

Every piece shows its traditional character and, around it, small marks that show how it moves, so you can play without reading the characters:

- a **dot** = one step that way; **two dots** in a row = two steps;
- an **arrow** = any distance that way;
- an **arrow with a bar across** = it jumps over one piece to capture;
- a **hollow dot** = allowed only after crossing the river.

| Piece | Marks | How it moves |
|---|---|---|
| General 帥 / 將 | 4 dots, straight | One step along a line, inside the palace. |
| Advisor 仕 / 士 | 4 dots, diagonal | One step diagonally, inside the palace. |
| Elephant 相 / 象 | 4 pairs of dots, diagonal | Exactly two points diagonally. It cannot cross the river and is blocked if the point in between (the "elephant's eye") is taken. |
| Horse 馬 | 8 dots | One point straight, then one diagonally outward. If the point next to it in that direction is taken, it cannot go that way ("hobbled horse"). |
| Chariot 車 | 4 arrows | Any distance along a line. The strongest piece. |
| Cannon 炮 / 砲 | 4 arrows with a bar | Moves like the chariot, but **captures** only by jumping over exactly one piece (of either side). |
| Soldier 兵 / 卒 | 1 dot forward, 2 hollow dots sideways | One step forward; after crossing the river also one step sideways. Never backward. |

Tap **Pieces** at any time to see this list with big pictures.

## Special rules

- **Generals never face each other** on a line with nothing between them. A move that would leave them facing is not allowed.
- **Check**: your general is attacked. You must get out of check.
- **Checkmate** wins. In Chinese chess, a player who has **no legal move at all** (even without being in check) also **loses**.

## Playing in the app

1. Tap one of your red pieces: green dots show where it may go (a green ring around an enemy piece means you can capture it). The status line says what the piece is and how it moves.
2. Tap a green dot to move.
3. **Undo** takes back your last move and the computer's answer. **Hint** shows a good move with an arrow.
4. The pieces each side has captured are shown above and below the board.

## Levels

- **Easy**: looks one move ahead and makes many mistakes. Good to learn.
- **Medium**: looks a few moves ahead and does not leave pieces hanging.
- **Hard**: thinks as deeply as it can in under a second.

## What is simplified

Official rules forbid perpetual check and perpetual chasing; here a repetition is simply a **draw**. The game is also a draw when the same position comes back **three times**, after **60 moves each without a capture**, or when neither side has a piece left that can attack (horse, chariot, cannon or soldier).

## Tips

- Bring out your chariots early: they are worth about two horses.
- The cannon needs a "screen" to capture; it is strongest at the start, when the board is full.
- Keep your advisors and elephants near the general: they are its bodyguards.

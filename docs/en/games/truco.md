# Truco

Truco is Brazil's favourite card game of bluff and good humour: win two of three tricks with the strongest cards, and shout **"Truco!"** to make the hand worth more. In Mili Games the app asks **How many at the table?** before each game: **2** (one against one) or **4** (two against two, partners sitting opposite each other). Against the computer, at four your partner is a computer too; you can also play with people **taking turns on one phone**.

## The cards
- 40 cards: a normal deck without the 8s, 9s, 10s and jokers. Everyone gets **3 cards**.
- Ordinary cards, strongest first: **3, 2, A, K, J, Q, 7, 6, 5, 4**. Suits do not matter for them: two 3s tie.
- The **manilhas** beat every ordinary card (they carry a small star in the app):
  - **Paulista:** after dealing, one card is turned face up — the **vira**. The next number up in the order above is the manilha (vira 7 → the queens; vira 3 → the 4s). Between manilhas the suit decides: **♣ "zap" > ♥ "copas" > ♠ "espadilha" > ♦ "pica-fumo" (ouros)**.
  - **Mineiro:** no vira; the manilhas are always **4♣ (zap) > 7♥ (copas) > A♠ (espadilha) > 7♦ (pica-fumo)**. The other 4s, 7s and aces are ordinary cards.

## A hand
- Each player plays one card per trick ("vaza"); the strongest card wins it and its player starts the next trick. Win **two tricks** to win the hand.
- **Ties:** if the first trick ties, the second decides; a tie later goes to whoever won the first; if the first two tie, the third decides; three ties and nobody scores.
- To play, tap a card (it rises) and tap it again, or press **Play card**.

## Truco, Seis, Nove, Doze
- A hand is worth **1 point**. On your turn, before playing, you may shout **Truco!** — the hand would be worth **3**.
- The other side answers with big buttons: **Accept**, **Run** (you score what the hand was worth before, 1) or raise: **Seis!** (6), then **Nove!** (9), then **Doze!** (12). After a raise is accepted, only the other side may raise again.
- The computer calls truco when its cards are good — and sometimes when they are not: it bluffs!

## Hand of eleven ("mão de onze")
When your side has **11 points**, you see your cards (and your partner's) before playing and decide: **play** (the hand is worth 3) or **run** (the others score 1). Nobody may shout truco in this hand. If both sides have 11, the last hand is played without truco.

The first side to **12 points** wins the game.

## Levels
- **Easy:** sometimes plays a random card, misjudges answers and rarely bluffs.
- **Medium:** plays sensibly — wins a trick as cheaply as possible, saves its cards when the partner is already winning — and answers by how strong its hand is.
- **Hard:** also weighs every card before playing, never runs when running would hand you the game, and bluffs more cleverly.

The computer never sees your cards: it judges its chances by imagining the cards it has not seen.

## Words
Truco keeps its Portuguese words in every language: **Truco!**, **Seis!** (six), **Nove!** (nine), **Doze!** (twelve), **manilha** (the trump cards), **vira** (the turned card), **zap** (the strongest manilha), **vaza** (a trick), **mão de onze** (hand of eleven).

## About regional rules
Truco changes from town to town. In Minas Gerais many tables start each hand at 2 points (truco 4, then 6 or 8, 10 and 12) and use a "hand of ten"; Mili Games uses the same simple 1-3-6-9-12 ladder and hand of eleven for both variants. Playing a card face down ("encoberta") and the partners' secret signals are not part of the app.

## Taking turns on one phone
Choose **Take turns on this phone** on the level screen, then 2 or 4 at the table: two people play one against one, or four people play in pairs (players 1 and 3 against players 2 and 4). After each move a cover hides the cards and asks to pass the phone to whoever must act next — to play a card, or to answer a truco (the player after the one who called answers). Play goes counterclockwise, as is the custom in Brazil. The score bar shows each side by its players and the symbols of their seats.

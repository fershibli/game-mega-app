# Sueca

Sueca is Portugal's favourite partnership card game, played in cafés and family gatherings: you and the partner sitting across from you try to collect more than half of the 120 points. In Mili Games you play with a **computer partner against two computer players**, or four people **take turns on one phone**.

## The cards
- 40 cards: a normal deck without the 8s, 9s and 10s. Everyone gets **10 cards**.
- In every suit, from strongest to weakest: **A, 7, K, J, Q, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2**.
- Points: **ace 11, seven 10, king 4, jack 3, queen 2**, the rest nothing — **120 points** in the deck.

## Trumps
The dealer's last card is shown to everyone (top-left corner, under **Trumps**): its suit is **trumps** for the whole hand, and the card stays in the dealer's hand. Trump cards carry a small star.

## Playing
- The player to the right of the dealer starts. Play goes to the right (counterclockwise), as in Portugal.
- **You must follow suit** when you can. If you cannot, play any card: a trump wins the trick, any other card is lost.
- The strongest trump takes the trick; without trumps, the strongest card of the suit led. Whoever takes it starts the next one.
- Cards you cannot play right now are greyed. Tap a card (it rises) and tap it again, or press **Play card**. **Hint** chooses a card for you.
- There is no Undo: in a card game everyone has already seen your card. The second tap is your safety net.

## Scoring
- **61 to 90 points:** your pair wins **1 game**.
- **91 or more:** **2 games**.
- **Every trick** (a *bandeira*): **4 games** — the match at once.
- **60 to 60:** a tie, nobody scores, and the next hand is worth **one game more**.
- The first pair to **4 games** wins the match.

## Your partner and the levels
The computer never looks at hidden cards: it only knows its own hand, the cards played and which suits someone has shown they lack.
- **Easy:** often plays a random (legal) card.
- **Medium:** plays like a good partner — when you are already winning a trick safely it does not overtake you and gives you its points instead ("carregar"), takes points as cheaply as it can and keeps its trumps.
- **Hard:** also imagines many possible deals of the unseen cards and plays each of its cards to the end of the hand, choosing the one that brings its side the most points.

## Taking turns
With **Take turns on this phone**, four people play (Players 1 and 3 against 2 and 4). Before each turn the screen covers the cards and asks to pass the phone; the next person taps the button and the table turns to their side.

## Tips
- Count the trumps that have gone, and the aces and sevens: a seven is the strongest card of its suit once the ace is out.
- When your partner is winning, give them a point card; when the opponents are winning, give your smallest card.

## About regional rules
In Brazil sueca is often played clockwise and some tables score differently; Mili Games uses the Portuguese direction and the 1-2-4 games scoring above. Signals between partners are not part of the app.

# Escova

Escova (also called escopa, the Brazilian and Portuguese cousin of the Italian scopa) is a calm game of adding up: play a card and take the cards on the table that add up to **15** with it. Before starting you choose **How many at the table?**: **2** (you against the computer) or **4** (you and a computer partner against two computers). People can also **take turns on one phone**.

## The cards
- 40 cards: a normal deck without the 8s, 9s and 10s.
- Values: **ace 1**, **2 to 7** their number, **jack 8**, **queen 9**, **king 10**. Every card shows its value in a dark circle.

## Playing
- Everyone gets **3 cards** and **4 cards** are laid face up on the table. If those four add up to 15 (or 30), the dealer takes them at once and scores one escova (two for 30).
- On your turn tap a card in your hand, then tap the table cards that make **15** with it. The total is shown in big numbers (for example **7 + 5 + 3 = 15 ✓**); press **Take**.
- A card that can make 15 **must take** (you choose which cards when there are several ways). A card that cannot is laid on the table with **Lay down**.
- Taking **every card on the table** is an **escova**: 1 point.
- When the hands are empty, everyone gets 3 more cards from the stock (none to the table). When the stock is out, whoever took last also takes what is left on the table (that is not an escova).
- **Clear** undoes your choice before you play; **Hint** shows a good move. Once played, a card cannot be taken back.

## Points at the end of each hand
- **1 point** for each escova.
- **1 point** for the most cards, **1** for the most diamonds (*ouros*), **1** for the **7♦** (*sete belo*), **1** for the most sevens. A tie gives the point to nobody.
- The first side to reach **15 points** at the end of a hand wins (the higher score if both reach it; another hand if they are equal).

## Levels
The computer sees only its own cards, the table and the cards already taken.
- **Easy:** takes the first thing it sees and lays cards down without thinking.
- **Medium:** weighs every move (sevens, diamonds and the 7♦ are worth more) and avoids leaving an easy escova.
- **Hard:** also counts which values are still unseen, to judge how likely the next player is to sweep the table.

## Tips
- After your move, try to leave the table adding up to more than 15, or to less than 5: then nobody can sweep it with one card.
- Keep an eye on the 7♦ and the other sevens.

## About regional rules
Some tables let you lay a card down even when it could take, play to 21 points or count the *primiera*; and some give the jack 9 and the queen 8. Mili Games uses the rules above.

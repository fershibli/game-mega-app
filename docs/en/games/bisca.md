# Bisca

Bisca is the quick two-player game of Portugal and Brazil, a cousin of the Italian briscola: three cards in hand, draw after every trick, and collect more than 60 of the 120 points. Before starting you choose **How many at the table?**: **2** (you against the computer) or **4** (you and a computer partner against two computers). People can also **take turns on one phone**.

## The cards
- 40 cards: a normal deck without the 8s, 9s and 10s. Everyone gets **3 cards**.
- In every suit, from strongest to weakest: **A, 7, K, J, Q, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2**. Points: **ace 11, seven 10, king 4, jack 3, queen 2** — **120 points** in all.
- After the deal one card is turned face up for **trumps** and put under the stock (top-left corner): it is the last card to be drawn. Trump cards carry a small star.

## Playing
- The player after the dealer starts. The strongest trump takes the trick; without trumps, the strongest card of the suit led.
- After each trick everybody **draws one card**, the winner of the trick first, and the winner starts the next trick.
- **While there are cards to draw, you may play any card** — no need to follow suit.
- **When the stock is empty you must follow suit** if you can (you do not have to beat the card or trump). Cards you cannot play are greyed.
- Tap a card (it rises) and tap it again, or press **Play card**. **Hint** chooses a card for you. There is no Undo: the other side has already seen your card.

## Scoring
- **61 points or more** wins **1 game**, **91 or more** wins **2 games**. 60 to 60 is a tie.
- The first side to **4 games** wins.

## Levels
The computer never looks at hidden cards or at the order of the stock.
- **Easy:** often plays a random card.
- **Medium:** plays small cards on empty tricks, takes aces and sevens as cheaply as it can, does not lead an ace you could trump for free, and helps its partner when playing in pairs.
- **Hard:** also imagines many orders of the unseen cards and plays every card to the end, keeping the one that brings the most points.

## Tips
- Keep your small trumps to catch the aces and sevens the other side plays.
- Lead small cards without points while the stock lasts; in the last tricks, count which aces and sevens are still out.

## About regional rules
Bisca changes from region to region: some tables play with 7 or 9 cards, exchange the 2 of trumps for the turned card, or do not have to follow suit even at the end. Mili Games uses three cards, no exchange, and following suit once the stock is gone.
